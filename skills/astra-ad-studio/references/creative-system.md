# Creative system

Use this reference to make the three creative directions strategically distinct and to choose the strongest one without flattening them into generic variations.

## Direction anatomy

Each direction must have a different central mechanism. Useful mechanisms include:

- **Pain interruption:** dramatize a recognizable frustrating moment.
- **Outcome reveal:** show the desired state first, then name the product that enables it.
- **Product proof:** make a real interface, workflow, transformation, or feature the hero.
- **Contrarian truth:** challenge an accepted belief with a defensible alternative.
- **Identity signal:** let the audience recognize the kind of person or team they want to be.
- **Visual metaphor:** express the value through one simple, ownable image.

Do not present three directions that share the same hook and layout with different colors.

## Scoring rubric

Score each dimension from 1 to 10 and add a brief reason based on the actual concept.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Stopping power | Would the first frame interrupt the target viewer? |
| Clarity | Can a stranger understand the product and benefit quickly? |
| Relevance | Does it speak to a specific audience situation or desire? |
| Credibility | Is the promise supported by the available product evidence? |
| Distinctiveness | Could a direct competitor plausibly run the same ad unchanged? |
| Buildability | Can the concept be executed well with the available assets and tools? |

Do not select solely by total score. A direction with slightly lower total but much stronger distinctiveness may be the better campaign anchor. State the tradeoff.

## Hook tests

A strong hook usually does one of the following within the first frame:

- names a painful or expensive situation;
- shows an unexpected product outcome;
- reveals a highly specific audience truth;
- creates an information gap that the next scene resolves;
- demonstrates the product doing useful work.

Reject hooks built only from generic praise, unexplained superlatives, or vague AI language.

## Copy hierarchy

For static ads, aim for:

- hook: 2–9 words;
- support line: one short sentence;
- CTA: 1–4 words;
- optional proof: one compact, verified fact.

For motion ads, keep each frame to one main idea. Spoken copy may add context, but the visual must remain understandable with sound off.
